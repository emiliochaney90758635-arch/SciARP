# Membrane-potential control as the leading ontology signal of ASXL1-mutated fibroblasts

Celeste R. Varga, Kenta Mori, Amina El-Amin, and Jonas Heller

## Abstract

ASXL1 disruption may alter the electrical and contractile programs of nonhematopoietic cells. We compared ASXL1-mutated and control primary fibroblasts by sex-adjusted RNA sequencing, removed samples with documented pharmacologic or alcohol-related confounding, and applied shrinkage to the disease-versus-control effect. Significant genes were analyzed against a complete human gene background for Biological Process enrichment and reduced by semantic similarity. Regulation of membrane potential retained the lowest adjusted enrichment probability and emerged as the unique leading process, whereas potassium ion transmembrane transport formed a closely related but statistically weaker term. Broader potassium transport, axon guidance, and contraction programs followed at higher adjusted probabilities. These data place membrane-potential regulation at the center of the ASXL1-associated fibroblast transcriptional phenotype.
