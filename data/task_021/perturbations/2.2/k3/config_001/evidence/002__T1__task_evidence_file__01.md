# Asymmetry of gene-level m6A gain and loss in bladder cancer

Ibrahim El-Sayed, Helena Krüger, Shiori Matsuda, and Malcolm Price

## Abstract

The balance between m6A gain and loss may shape post-transcriptional regulation in bladder cancer. We analyzed a transcript-resolved MeRIP result table and formed separate gene-symbol sets from records classified as significantly hypermethylated or hypomethylated. Multiple transcripts bearing the same gene symbol were counted once within each class. We identified 184 hypermethylated genes and 156 hypomethylated genes, corresponding to a Hyper:Hypo fold ratio of 1.18. Overlap between the classes was limited and did not materially alter the class-specific counts. The modest excess of m6A gain suggests a comparatively balanced epitranscriptomic remodeling program rather than a strongly hypermethylation-dominated landscape.
