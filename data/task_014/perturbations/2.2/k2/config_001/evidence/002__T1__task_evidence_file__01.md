# cGMP signaling dominates Reactome enrichment after the second chronic T-cell challenge

Lucía Moret, Haruto Ishida, Nneka Afolayan, and Erik Sandström

## Abstract

Repeated T-cell challenge can expose signaling programs that are not apparent in single-round CRISPR activation screens. We selected chronic round-2 candidates by the lower MAGeCK probability across two replicates, mapped RefSeq identifiers to human gene symbols, and performed Reactome over-representation analysis against all measured mapped genes. Duplicate pathway-library loads were collapsed by stable Reactome identifier before correction and ranking. The cGMP effects pathway retained the smallest adjusted probability and was uniquely more significant than nitric oxide stimulation of guanylate cyclase. The signal was supported by coordinated representation of cyclic-nucleotide phosphodiesterases, kinase effectors, and potassium-channel regulators. These results nominate cGMP effector signaling as the leading round-2 pathway, while the replicate-minimum candidate rule limits the finding to exploratory prioritization.
