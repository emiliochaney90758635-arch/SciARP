# Persistent cyclic-nucleotide pathway enrichment across chronic T-cell challenges

Fatima Zahiri, Lukas Brenner, Seung-A Cho, and Matthew J. Ingram

## Abstract

Pathways that recur across successive immune challenges may mark durable regulators of T-cell evasion. We analyzed three rounds of a chronic CRISPR activation screen by selecting RefSeq candidates from the smaller of two replicate MAGeCK probabilities, mapping candidates to human gene symbols, and testing Reactome pathways against a shared measured-gene background. Duplicate records originating from repeated loading of the pathway library were consolidated by canonical identifier. The cGMP effects pathway passed adjusted significance in rounds 1, 2, and 3 and was the only canonical pathway shared by all three significant sets. Nitric oxide stimulation of guanylate cyclase was restricted to round 2. The persistent cGMP signal suggests a stable cyclic-nucleotide component across chronic exposure, although minimum-replicate selection makes the analysis exploratory.
