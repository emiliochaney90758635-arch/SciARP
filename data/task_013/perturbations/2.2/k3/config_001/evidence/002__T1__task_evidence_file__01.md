# Progressive Reactome remodeling across repeated chronic T-cell exposure

Marina Kovács, Daisuke Watanabe, Selam Gebre, and Hugh L. Mercer

## Abstract

Repeated immune-cell exposure can shift the pathway landscape recovered from pooled activation screens. We compared three chronic-exposure rounds from a genome-wide T-cell CRISPR activation dataset. For each RefSeq entry and round, the smaller of two replicate MAGeCK probabilities was used for exploratory candidate selection at 0.05, mapped gene symbols were tested against a common measured-gene background, and Reactome results were corrected for multiple testing. After canonical pathway deduplication, round 3 yielded six significant pathways, round 2 yielded two, and round 1 yielded none. The round-3 set included vesicle trafficking, cytokine signaling, and nucleotide-metabolism programs, indicating that the strongest pathway-level response emerged after the final repeated exposure. Minimum-replicate selection increases entry opportunities, so these findings describe an exploratory screen rather than confirmatory evidence.
