# Expected Patient Interaction and the Ordinal Burden of Adverse Events

Lena M. Forsberg, Daniel K. Owusu, Aisha Rahman, and Peter J. Calver

## Abstract

Anticipated patient interaction may mark occupational conditions associated with adverse-event reporting and severity among healthcare workers. Trial participants were reduced to one record using the highest recorded severity across all adverse-event types. Complete cases were analyzed with a proportional-odds logistic regression including randomized treatment, expected patient interaction, and an integer-coded three-level patient-volume covariate. Expected interaction was contrasted as Yes versus No. Expected interaction was associated with increased odds of occupying a higher maximum-severity category (adjusted odds ratio 1.31; nominal p=0.038). The finding is associative and should not be interpreted as a causal effect.
